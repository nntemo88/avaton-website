The challenges

Why factory AI projects stall

Data trapped in silos

Production data is scattered across MES, PLM, SAP and the shop floor, so no single system can reason about the whole operation.

Inspection cannot keep pace

Manual quality checks cannot keep up with line speed, so defects slip through and only samples ever get inspected.

Slow to plan and reprogram

Digital twins and robot reprogramming take weeks of specialist effort, so every layout change or new task is a project.

What we deploy

Agents for manufacturing

Concrete agents, deployed and governed, mapped to the work that matters on the factory floor.

Digital factory simulation

A crew of agents turns real factory scans and blueprints into a complete, navigable 3D twin, then tests layout and flow variants against safety and throughput before anything moves on the floor.

A new factory layout in minutes, not weeks.

Self-teaching robotics

The agent teaches a robot arm new handling and picking skills and writes and runs the control code itself, so a fresh task no longer means weeks of manual programming.

Robots learn new tasks with effectively zero lines of code.

Quality inspection

Vision agents check every part on the line in real time, flag defects with high precision and route them for action, rather than relying on spot checks of a few samples.

100% of parts inspected on the line, not just samples.

Predictive maintenance

Agents watch machine and sensor data for early signs of wear, predict failures before they happen and schedule maintenance around production, so equipment is fixed before it stops the line.

Less unplanned downtime and longer asset life.

Document processing and back-office

Agents read blueprints, work instructions and supplier paperwork, extract the rules and data hidden inside and turn orders, invoices and quality records into validated structured data across systems.

Shop-floor knowledge becomes searchable and reusable, with no manual data entry.

Supply and demand planning

Aligns production plans with live demand, supplier and inventory signals, so the plant builds what is needed and flags shortages before they stop the line.

Fewer stoppages and less excess stock, with plans that adjust to reality.
